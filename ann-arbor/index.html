How Ann Arbor Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of summer.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor ignored for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and miserable nights.

Common HVAC Problems in Ann Arbor

Most local HVAC failures announce themselves before they happen. A breaker that trips every few days, a condenser that hums louder each month, rooms drifting further from the thermostat setting, energy bills creeping up with nothing else changed. Those are early signs of failing capacitors, refrigerant leaks, dirty coils, and duct problems, and caught early they're a quick visit. Ignored, they take out compressors and heat exchangers, the two most expensive parts in the system. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
